# P06 · Client email triage

**Current version:** v1.2 · **Status:** Tested · **Workflow stage:** Communication 1 · **Feeds into:** Routes to staff

> Sample portfolio for BUS4005 Assessment 1. Harbourside Accounting and all figures are invented. Use the structure, not the content.

## Prompt text

```text
You are the front-desk assistant at Harbourside Accounting. Triage this client
email. Subject: [SUBJECT]. Body: [BODY].
Categories (choose one): DOCUMENT_HELP, APPOINTMENT_CHANGE, RETURN_STATUS,
FEE_QUERY, ATO_LETTER_RECEIVED, TAX_QUESTION, COMPLAINT, OTHER.
Reply with JSON only: {"category":"", "urgency":"high|normal|low",
"summary":"max 25 words", "route_to":"front_desk|accountant|partner",
"deadline_mentioned":""}
Rules: ATO_LETTER_RECEIVED and COMPLAINT are always "high". TAX_QUESTION and
ATO_LETTER_RECEIVED go to "accountant"; COMPLAINT goes to "partner". Copy any
date the client mentions, otherwise "none". Do not reply to the client.
```

## Workflow / task

Client communication, step 1. Runs on every email to the shared inbox and sets the tag and the person responsible.

## Problem solved

150+ emails a day in peak season. Urgent ones (e.g. an ATO letter with a due date) sit unread among routine questions; sorting takes the front desk about 1.5 hours a day.

## Automation potential

Very high. Rule-based and repetitive; JSON sets tags automatically. Staff correct any misrouted email and corrections are reviewed weekly.

## Risks & limitations

Urgent email rated low and missed → fixed urgency rules, any date mentioned is flagged. Clients writing in a second language misread as complaints → partner reviews complaints, weekly sample check.

## Version history

| Version | Change made | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1.0 | Categories only | An ATO letter with a due date rated “normal” | Important cases need fixed rules |
| v1.1 | Fixed urgency and routing rules | 6 of 6 ATO letters rated high and routed to an accountant | Rules beat judgement for high-risk cases |
| v1.2 | Added deadline capture and “do not reply to the client” | 3 of 40 misrouted, all low-urgency | Measure errors by impact, not just count |

## Test evidence

40 sample emails, including 6 ATO letters: all 6 rated high and routed to an accountant. 3 of 40 were misrouted, all low-urgency.
