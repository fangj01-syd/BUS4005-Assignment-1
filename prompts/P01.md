# P01 · Welcome email and document checklist

**Current version:** v1.2 · **Status:** Tested · **Workflow stage:** Onboarding 1 · **Feeds into:** P02

> Sample portfolio for BUS4005 Assessment 1. Harbourside Accounting and all figures are invented. Use the structure, not the content.

## Prompt text

```text
You are a client services officer at Harbourside Accounting, a small Australian
accounting practice. Write a welcome email to [CLIENT_FIRST_NAME], who has
booked us to prepare their [FINANCIAL_YEAR] tax return.
Client type: [CLIENT_TYPE] (salary only / salary + rental / sole trader).
Accountant: [ACCOUNTANT_NAME]. Appointment: [APPOINTMENT_DATE].
Include: a 1–2 sentence thank-you; a bullet checklist using ONLY the list for
their client type in [CHECKLIST_TABLE]; how to upload via [PORTAL_LINK]
(never by email); the appointment date and accountant's name.
Under 200 words, friendly Australian English. Do not give tax advice, estimate
a refund, or ask for TFNs or bank details by email.
```

## Workflow / task

Onboarding, step 1. Runs when a client books online; the client services officer checks and sends it the same day.

## Problem solved

The officer writes this email about 900 times a season, around 10 minutes each. Incomplete checklists are the main reason clients arrive without documents and appointments are rescheduled.

## Automation potential

High. Same structure every time and all inputs are in the booking system. About 10 → 2 minutes per client, roughly 120 staff hours a season (estimate).

## Risks & limitations

Wrong checklist for unusual clients (e.g. foreign income) → only three client types in scope, others written manually. Tax advice slipping in → explicit exclusion plus human check. Clients emailing sensitive data → email points to the secure portal.

## Version history

| Version | Change made | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1.0 | “Write a welcome email for a new tax client.” | Generic email, no checklist, US spelling | Needs a role and the client’s details |
| v1.1 | Added role, [PLACEHOLDERS] and [CHECKLIST_TABLE] | Right checklist in 6 of 6; one draft estimated a refund | State what the AI must not do |
| v1.2 | Added: no tax advice, no refund estimate, no TFN or bank details by email | 6 of 6 usable after a quick check | Exclusions belong in the prompt, not just the review |

## Test evidence

Ran on 6 sample bookings (2 per client type). All 6 used the right checklist. In v1.1 one draft estimated a refund; the v1.2 rule fixed it.
